## Article 1: Best Printer Tables for Home Offices

### Content Gaps
- Nobody measures common printer model dimensions against stand tabletop size (e.g. a compact inkjet AIO at ~14.8"x13.7" vs a business AIO at ~17.5"x15.4"). This "will my printer fit" question is the #1 unanswered question in reviews.
- No clear distinction between printer table (tall fixed furniture), printer stand (small compact platform), and printer cart (mobile) — buyers searching "printer table" often actually need a cart.
- Ergonomic height is never addressed: output tray should sit roughly 28-32 inches from the floor so paper can be grabbed without excessive bending.
- Back clearance for cables (power, USB, ethernet) is rarely mentioned — stands pushed flush to a wall can leave no room to plug in.
- "Easy assembly" claims rarely disclose real time (often 45-90 minutes) or that a separate screwdriver is needed.
- Weight capacity is usually listed as a single total figure, not broken down per shelf — a stand holding a printer plus a ream of paper (~5kg) plus spare ink plus files can total 20-30kg, and per-shelf distribution matters.

## Article 2: Best Printer Stands with Storage

- Drawer depth vs. paper ream size is never compared: a standard letter ream (21.6 x 27.9 cm) needs at least ~30cm of drawer depth to lie flat; many budget stands have 25-28cm drawers where the ream doesn't fully fit.

- No guidance on open shelf (easy access, dust exposure) vs. closed drawer (cleaner, less visible) trade-offs by use case.
- Cable management inside the storage unit (grommet holes, routing) is never evaluated.
- Material and moisture resistance (MDF/particleboard swelling near windows or humid rooms vs. solid wood/engineered wood with veneer) is never discussed in a printer-stand context.

## Article 4: Best Under-Desk Printer Stands

- No fit-test guidance combining desk clearance (typically 24" standard desk, 27"+ standing desk lowest, 28-30" adjustable) with stand height (~14") plus printer height (~10-15"), which can total 24-29" and simply not fit under many desks.

- Dust accumulation and ventilation (especially relevant for laser printers that need airflow to dissipate heat) under an enclosed desk is never addressed.

## Article 5: Best Rolling Printer Stands

### Content Gaps
- Caster material is never tested for floor impact: plastic wheels on hard floors can scratch/mark, while rubber wheels don't.
- Rolling stability while actively printing isn't tested — vibration during printing combined with a poorly-locked caster can cause slight drift that strains the power/data cable.
- Carpet vs. hard floor rolling performance (carpet resistance makes pushing harder) is never noted.
- Locking caster effectiveness varies — a weak lock can still drift when the printer is bumped or when print buttons are pressed — and is never rated.
- Cable length relative to rolling distance is never discussed — a short cable limits how far the stand can actually roll before the cord goes taut.

## Article 6: Best Desktop Printer Stands

### Content Gaps
- Vibration stability while printing is never tested — thin acrylic risers can wobble more than bamboo or metal risers.
- Real printer weight limits vs. riser capacity are never cross-referenced: acrylic risers commonly rated around 22lbs, while a heavier all-in-one laser printer can weigh 40+ lbs and simply not fit safely.
- Heat dissipation is ignored: acrylic/glass risers trap heat poorly compared to open designs, which matters for laser printers radiating heat downward and can affect printer longevity.
- The optimal lift height for tray access at eye/reach level while standing or sitting is never calculated — too much lift can make the paper tray uncomfortably high, too little defeats the purpose.
- For risers with a storage drawer underneath, drawer clearance when the printer overhangs the front edge is never tested — an overhanging printer can block the drawer from opening fully.

## Article 7: Best Printer Cabinets for Home Offices

### Content Gaps
- Ventilation is the single most important and least-discussed factor: laser printers generate heat and ozone, inkjets need air circulation, and a sealed cabinet can cause overheating, shorten printer lifespan, and (for laser printers specifically) create an ozone buildup health concern.
- Door-open clearance while actively printing is never addressed: closing the cabinet door during printing can cause jams or block output access, but that conflicts with the cabinet's whole point of hiding the printer — a "cabinet with a paper passthrough slot" design solves this and is never suggested.
- Cable routing inside a cabinet (where cables enter/exit, whether there's a grommet hole in back) is never shown.
- Interior height requirements for an all-in-one with a scanner lid that opens upward (needing roughly 12-15 extra inches of clearance) are never calculated.
- Noise containment is claimed but never quantified — an enclosed cabinet might reduce a 40-55 dB laser printer by only 5-10 dB, and no guide tests or estimates this.

### WorkCocoon Differentiators
- Ventilation requirement by printer type: does the cabinet need a vent slot?
- Health note on laser-printer ozone buildup in sealed cabinets
- Door design comparison: open-front vs. passthrough-slot door
- Interior height = printer height + scanner lid clearance
- Noise reduction estimate: enclosed vs. open
- Cable routing/grommet location guide

## Article 8: Best Printer Stands for Two Printers

### Content Gaps
- Tier placement for stability is never guided: the heavier printer (typically laser) belongs on the wider, lower tier for a lower center of gravity, while the lighter printer (inkjet) goes up top — nobody explains this.
- Access ergonomics for stacked printers are rarely discussed honestly: a bottom-tier printer requires bending to load paper, while a top-tier printer requires reaching up.
- Cable management for two printers (2 power cables, 2 USB or ethernet, possibly 2 network cables) is never shown as a combined routing solution.
- Weight capacity is usually given as a single total figure, not per-shelf — a stand rated 100lbs total might have a top shelf rated for only 30lbs, which a heavier laser printer could exceed.
- Vibration transfer between two printers on the same stand when both print simultaneously, and whether that affects print quality, is never addressed.

## Article 9: Best Printers for Home Use in 2026

### Content Gaps
- Total cost of ownership over 3-5 years is rarely calculated with real volume assumptions: a cheap printer plus high-volume ink costs can total far more over several years than a higher-upfront-cost ink-tank printer with cheap refills.
- Ink drying out from infrequent home use is a real, unquantified cost: inkjet cartridges can dry out after 2-4 weeks idle, leading to 1-2 wasted cartridges per year from auto-cleaning cycles and dried ink, purely from occasional use.

- Noise level in decibels is essentially never compared, despite mattering for printing late at night near sleeping people.

## Article 20: Best Easy-to-Use Printers

### Content Gaps
- "Easy to use" is never broken into its actual components: easy setup, easy daily use, easy maintenance (ink changes, jam clearing), and easy troubleshooting are different things that get lumped together.
- Setup experience for elderly or non-tech users (WPS button pairing, forced app downloads, account creation) is never rated from that specific perspective.
- Paper jam clearing complexity (some printers have generous door access, others require reaching into awkward spots) is never compared.
- Ink change process complexity differs a lot: cartridge swaps take 2-3 minutes, while ink-tank refills (unscrewing caps, pouring without spilling) take 5-10 minutes — this is never rated.
- Error message clarity (a cryptic error code vs. a plain-language message like "Paper Jam in Rear Tray — Open Door B") is never compared.
- Companion app quality (some apps are poorly rated with forced account creation and bugs, others are well-reviewed) is never included in an "easy to use" assessment.

## Top Gaps Across All 20 Articles

1. **Real dimension/fit-test data** — printer dimensions vs. stand size, desk clearance vs. stand+printer height, footprint vs. desk width. Competitors rarely publish these numbers even though they answer the #1 buyer question.
2. **Honest volume-based segmentation** — most "best printer for X" content ignores that the right printer depends heavily on actual monthly print volume, which varies wildly by use case (10 pages/month vs. 1000+ for homeschooling).
3. **Real cost-over-time math** — TCO tables, ink dry-out costs, cost-per-page with the OEM/third-party toner DRM caveat, and payback calculations for ink-tank printers are almost universally missing from affiliate content.
