## Article 1: Best Murphy Beds with Desks

### Content Gaps
**1. Desk Weight Limit: Items Left on Desk When Lowering**
- Most murphy bed desks recommend keeping items under 40 lbs on the desk surface when lowering. Tall items (monitors, lamps over 16") must be removed. No guide specifies max item height that can remain on the desk.

**2. Spring vs. Piston Mechanism: Long-Term Reliability**
- Piston (gas strut): smoother, but loses pressure over years, must be replaced entirely (~$100-200/pair). Spring: adjustable tension, rebuildable, serviceable long-term but quality varies. For daily use, spring is better for long-term serviceability.

**3. Mattress Thickness Limits the Sleeping Experience**
- Most murphy bed desks accommodate mattresses 10-12" thick max. 8-9" recommended if using linens/made bed without clearing.

**4. Outlet and Cable Management**
- Wall outlet behind the cabinet is blocked after installation. Solution: run an outlet extension cord through baseboard hole during installation. Most buyers discover this after installation.

**5. Room Clearance When Bed Is Down**
- Desk cannot be used while bed is down. Room needs 85-90" clear floor space from the wall. No guide maps room clearance requirements.

### WorkCocoon Differentiators
- Desk item height limit during bed lowering; spring vs. piston longevity; mattress thickness guide (10" vs 12"); outlet planning checklist; room depth requirement.

## Article 2: Best Filing Cabinets for Home Offices

### Content Gaps
**1. Steel Gauge** — 18-gauge = heaviest/dent-resistant (premium); 20-gauge = standard mid-range; 22-24-gauge = budget, dents easily. Rarely disclosed.

**2. Full-Extension vs. 3/4-Extension Drawers** — 3/4-extension leaves back files inaccessible without pulling forward. Full-extension slides entirely out.

**3. Anti-Tip Interlock** — Prevents multiple drawers opening at once (tip-over risk). ANSI/BIFMA requires it on cabinets 28"+ tall. Budget imports often skip it.

**4. Assembly Reality** — Many "metal" cabinets are partially MDF/particle board with metal face. True all-metal is heavier, minimal assembly.

**5. Hanging File Compatibility by Drawer Size** — Letter drawers: 12-13" internal width. Legal: 15-16". Some "legal-compatible" cabinets need an adapter rail.

### WorkCocoon Differentiators
- Steel gauge guide; full-ext vs 3/4-ext explanation; anti-tip interlock safety check; all-metal vs hybrid distinction; hanging file size compatibility.

## Article 3: Best Murphy Beds for Small Spaces

### Content Gaps
**1. Vertical vs. Horizontal Ceiling Height Decision** — Vertical queen: ~88" cabinet, needs 8' ceiling min (93" with anchor clearance). Horizontal queen: ~66-70", works with lower/sloped ceilings.

**2. Floor Clearance When Open** — Vertical queen projects 85-90" from wall. Minimum recommended room depth: 13-14 ft.

**3. Wall Structure Requirement** — Must anchor to studs (16" on center, span 2+ studs), not drywall. Masonry/metal stud walls need special anchors.

**4. Door Swing Conflicts** — Open bed occupies the floor space where an inward-swinging door travels; may need to rehang door outward.

**5. Heating/Cooling Vents** — Floor/wall vents behind or beside the cabinet get blocked; must reroute before installation.

## Article 4: Best Office Chairs for Long Hours

### Content Gaps
**1. Seat Depth Adjustment** — Correct depth: 2-4 fingers between knee back and seat front. Many "ergonomic" chairs lack this adjustment (only seat height adjusts).

**2. Heat Buildup: Mesh vs. Foam Seat Pan** — Mesh back matters less than seat pan material for heat; foam seat pan traps heat after 2-3 hrs regardless of mesh back. Breathable seat pan (perforated foam, mesh seat) is cooler.

**3. Lumbar Adjustment Range: Position AND Pressure** — Cheap = fixed foam bump. Good = adjustable height. Premium = height + depth/firmness.

**4. Seat Width vs. Hip Width** — Standard seat: 18-20". Plus-size chairs: 21-24" needed above ~250 lbs.

**5. Recline Tension Adjustment** — Too light = unexpected recline; too heavy = can't recline at all. Rarely explained how to set correctly for body weight.

## Article 5: Best Filing Cabinets for Small Spaces

### Content Gaps
**1. Footprint vs. Access Radius** — A 2-drawer vertical cabinet (15"x20") needs 38-40" total depth clearance when drawer is open. Lateral cabinets open sideways, need less depth but more width.

**4. Depth vs. File Orientation** — Some compact cabinets only fit front-to-back files, not proper side-to-side hanging files.

**5. Weight and Moving** — Loaded 2-drawer cabinet: 60-100 lbs. For carpet, look for 2.5"+ diameter casters.

## Article 16: Best Locking Filing Cabinets

### Content Gaps
**1. Locks Are Privacy Devices, Not Security** — Standard cam locks defeated in seconds; real security needs a fireproof UL-rated safe.

**2. Key Duplication Risk** — COM (common) keys mean one key often opens many same-brand cabinets; replacement keys ordered online with just a key number.

**3. UL-Listed vs. Standard Cam Locks** — UL Group 2 resists 20 hrs of expert manipulation; appropriate minimum for tax/legal documents.

**4. Bar Lock vs. Individual Drawer Lock** — Bar lock locks all drawers at once (single point of failure); individual locks are more granular.

**5. Lock Replacement Availability** — Proprietary budget locks may become unavailable in 3-5 years, requiring drill-out ($50 locksmith).

**1. Recline Lock Positions: Working vs Resting** — Working recline: 100-110°. Break: 120-130°. Near-nap: 135-140°. Fixed-position chairs may skip the working angle entirely.

**5. Weight Limit in Reclined vs. Upright Position** — Some manufacturers rate reclined position lower (e.g., 300 lbs upright, 250 lbs at full recline).

## Article 23: Best Metal Filing Cabinets

### Content Gaps
**1. Steel Gauge Guide** — 18-gauge (premium, ~0.048") vs 20-gauge (mid, ~0.036") vs 22-24-gauge (budget, ~0.030", visible flex). Rarely disclosed; press-test method explained.

**2. All-Welded vs. Knockdown (KD) Construction** — KD ships flat/bolts together, weaker joints under heavy file loads vs factory-welded.

**3. Powder Coat Finish Quality** — Thin coat chips at drawer edges within 1-2 yrs; visual test at corners reveals quality.

**4. All-Metal vs. Wood-Accented Hybrid** — Wood elements can warp/swell in humid environments (basements, garages).

**5. Off-Gassing/VOC Consideration** — Powder-coated all-welded cabinets can off-gas more than laminate furniture — relevant for sensitive users.

### WorkCocoon Differentiators
- Steel gauge guide with press-test method; welded vs KD distinction; powder coat visual quality test; all-metal vs hybrid for humid environments; off-gassing consideration.

## Article 24: Best Room Dividers for Home Offices

### Content Gaps
**1. Echo Reduction vs. Sound Transmission** — Fabric screens absorb reflections (reduce echo) but do NOT block sound between rooms; true blocking needs mass + air gap (solid partition).

**2. Freestanding Screen Stability** — Zig-zag panel arrangement needed for self-support; rubber non-slip feet minimum; wall-anchor D-rings for households with pets/kids.

**3. Floor-Level Privacy Gap** — Typical 1-2" gap at bottom lets pets/draft/light through; floor-length fabric dividers eliminate this but need precise measurement.

**4. Video Call Background Quality** — Busy patterns distract on camera; solid color positioned 1-2 ft behind the user is ideal; backlit screens create silhouette issues.

**5. Freestanding vs. Ceiling-Mounted vs. Tension Rod** — Trade-off between flexibility (freestanding, max 5-6 ft height) and permanence/acoustic separation (ceiling track, drill required) vs no-drill tension rod (up to 8-10 ft in narrow spaces).
