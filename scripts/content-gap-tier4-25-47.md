## Article 25: Best Cube Storage Room Dividers

## Article 26: Best Office Chairs with Headrests
### Content Gaps
- Headrest position differs for upright vs. reclined use; fixed headrests often serve only one position well.
- Universal clip-on aftermarket headrests ($60-80) are a legitimate alternative to chairs with poor built-in headrests.
- Headrest protrusion (1-4") can worsen forward head posture if too deep.
- Headrest width (4-6" narrow vs 8-12" wide) affects lateral neck support.
- Headrest material: foam/leather traps heat vs mesh which breathes better.
### WorkCocoon Differentiators
Upright vs reclined headrest position guide; aftermarket headrest option; protrusion depth guidance; width for lateral support; material heat comparison.

## Article 27: Best Office Chairs for Heavy People
### Content Gaps
- Dynamic vs static weight capacity: buy 50 lbs above actual body weight (dynamic impact adds 30-50%).
- Frame steel gauge: heavier empty chair weight (40-55 lbs) suggests heavier-gauge steel.
- Cylinder class: Class 4 for heavy users (250+ lbs) vs standard Class 3 which fails faster.
- Seat foam density (2.5 lb+) matters more than "thick padding" for long-term support.
- Warranty weight limit stated in warranty doc may be lower than marketed capacity — verify both.
### WorkCocoon Differentiators
50 lb buffer rule; steel gauge/empty-weight proxy; cylinder class explained; foam density guide; warranty weight limit vs marketing claim distinction.

## Article 31: Best Standing Desks with Keyboard Trays
### Content Gaps
- Negative tilt (not flat, not positive) is the ergonomically correct keyboard angle; most budget trays lack it.
- Mouse platform should sit at same height as keyboard; left-hand availability is rare.
- Keyboard tray reduces usable desk depth by 6-8" — significant on desks under 24" deep.
- Track extension length (18-24") must match user's typical sitting distance from the desk edge.
- Weight rating should account for lean pressure (20-25 lb minimum), not just keyboard+mouse weight.
### WorkCocoon Differentiators
Negative vs flat vs positive tilt guide; mouse platform height/handedness; usable surface reduction calculation; track extension vs sitting distance; lean-pressure weight rating recommendation.

## Article 37: Best Freestanding Murphy Beds
### Content Gaps
- "Freestanding" means no wall MOUNT, not no anchor at all — floor/wall cleat anchors are often still required.
- Floor anchoring may violate rental lease terms just like wall drilling — the "renter-friendly" claim is often overstated.
- Cabinet base weight is a stability proxy; lighter cabinets risk tipping during mid-deployment.
- Freestanding units still need wall backing plus 85-90" open floor clearance — not truly "anywhere in the room."
- Mechanisms are often calibrated for lighter/thinner mattresses (8-10"); thick 12" foam may exceed calibration.
### WorkCocoon Differentiators
Anchor requirement truth explained; renter lease compliance reality check; cabinet base weight as stability proxy; room layout/wall backing clarification; mattress weight/thickness compatibility.

## Article 39: Best Filing Cabinets for Legal-Size Documents

- Hanging file rails must be spaced for legal (14.25" apart) — letter-spaced rails need separate adapters/replacement.
- Drawer depth for front-to-back legal files needs verification; full-extension slides matter more at this length.

- Lateral cabinets are a rarely-suggested but often better alternative to vertical for legal-size storage.

Internal vs external width distinction; hanging rail spacing compatibility; drawer depth/slide-extension check; slide load rating recommendation; lateral vs vertical alternative for legal docs.

## Article 42: Best Fireproof Filing Cabinets for Home Offices
### Content Gaps
- UL Class 350 (paper) vs Class 150 (digital media) — most home cabinets are 350 only, inadequate for USB drives/discs.
- "Fireproof" without UL/ETL certification is an unverified marketing claim.
- Fire duration ratings (30 min/1 hr/2 hr) should map to realistic residential fire response scenarios.
- Water resistance (from fire suppression) is a separate, often-missing protection dimension from fire rating alone.
- Fireproof cabinets are 2-4x heavier than standard metal cabinets — matters for upper-floor placement.
### WorkCocoon Differentiators
Class 350 vs 150 explained; certification-first filter; duration rating vs realistic scenario; water resistance as separate need; empty weight/upper-floor placement disclosure.

## Article 44: Best Room Dividers on Wheels
### Content Gaps
- Caster load rating (often only 20-30 lbs) should be checked against actual panel weight.
- Caster material must match floor type (rubber for hardwood, larger diameter for carpet).
- Tall dividers (72"+) are less stable when rolling and require careful tilt; shorter designs are safer.
- Rolling repositioning can be noisy on hardwood with nylon casters; rubber/felt is quieter.
- Locking caster reliability degrades with plastic levers; metal-lever locks last longer.

Caster load vs panel weight; material-per-floor guide; rolling stability by height; repositioning noise comparison; locking caster durability (metal vs plastic).

## Article 45: Best Double-Sided Bookshelves
### Content Gaps
- Open back allows visibility/light through both sides — not true privacy; closed back needed for that.
- Freestanding tall units (72"+) need ceiling or floor anchoring since no wall is available for center-of-room placement.

## Article 46: Best No-Drill Room Dividers for Renters
### Content Gaps
- Tension poles can crack ceilings if over-tightened — a real security-deposit risk.
- Tension pole min/max extension range must match exact ceiling height; non-standard heights may not be compatible.
- Curtain weight limit on tension poles (4-10 lbs) is easy to exceed with heavy/blackout fabric.
- Folding screens need zig-zag panel arrangement for stability — arranged straight, they fall over easily.
- Portability/transport dimensions matter for renters who move frequently.
### WorkCocoon Differentiators
Ceiling damage warning + correct tension guide; ceiling height range compatibility; curtain weight limit; zig-zag stability guide; portability/transport dimension disclosure.

## Article 47: Best Tall Room Dividers for High Ceilings
### Content Gaps
- Freestanding tall dividers (90-96") are inherently top-heavy and tip-prone without anchoring.
- Ceiling track curtain systems are a better (if more permanent) solution for high ceilings than freestanding screens.
- Freestanding dividers under 8' in a 9-10' room leave an acoustic gap — visual privacy only, no real sound blocking.
- Tall dividers placed in HVAC airflow paths can create pressure imbalance/stuffiness on one side.
- Heavy acoustic panel material (PET felt) is more effective at mid-frequency sound than lightweight foam, but much heavier to install/move.
### WorkCocoon Differentiators
Stability/anchoring for 90"+ screens; ceiling track as primary high-ceiling recommendation; acoustic gap disclosure; airflow positioning guidance; material density vs noise frequency matching.
